Identity guide · first edition · November 2026

Majed & Mohamed Alzayed Trading and Industry Co.
01 · The idea

Where two great strokes meet.

For three decades, Alzayed has stood where things meet: the world’s great names and the Syrian market, European wardrobes and Syrian sewing floors, cloth and bread, industry and property. The identity draws that meeting in two letters.

The A and the Z are the first and the defining letter of the name. Cut in a classical Caslon, the A’s heavy right leg and the Z’s heavy diagonal meet at a single point on the baseline: two strong strokes from two directions, one foundation. Around them, a quiet system drawn from cloth and metal: claret wool, brass, ivory cotton, cypress green; a serif with three centuries of printing behind it; and room to breathe.

TimelessA classical letterform, not a trend. Nothing in the system dates it.
TheirsTheir initials and their two languages, set as equals.
StrongA monogram that holds at 16 pixels and on a building’s façade.
02 · The monogram

A and Z, joined on the baseline.

Drawn, not typedBoth letters are Libre Caslon Display’s own outlines (SIL Open Font License), the Z set 120 units into the A and the two united into one shape.
The meeting pointThe dashed lines cross where the A’s right leg lands and the Z’s diagonal begins: the one detail that makes the pair a mark.
Small-size cutBelow 64 px (favicons, app icons) a second master thickens every hairline by 2.6 % so the letters survive.
03 · The lockups

The name in two scripts, as equals.

Primary: the bilingual stack. Then the English stack, the Arabic stack and the two wordmarks alone. The monogram never stands in a row beside the name.

04 · Space and size

Room to breathe.

Clear spaceOn every side, at least x: the height of ALZAYED’s capitals in the lockup.
Smallest sizesStack 120 px / 30 mm wide. Wordmark 90 px / 22 mm. Monogram 16 px with the small-size cut.
GroundsIvory, paper, claret, oxblood, ink. On photographs, only over a calm area, in ivory or brass.
05 · Colour

Claret, brass and ivory.

Claret#4A1420 · RGB 74, 20, 32 CMYK, approx. 0 · 73 · 57 · 71the house colour. Deep red wool, the lining of a jewel box, the evening.
Oxblood#2B0B12 · RGB 43, 11, 18 CMYK, approx. 0 · 74 · 58 · 83claret in shadow, for the deepest grounds and the footer.
Brass#C2A36B · RGB 194, 163, 107 CMYK, approx. 0 · 16 · 45 · 24the metal. The monogram, rules and figures on claret and ink, never small text on ivory.
Ivory#F5EFE4 · RGB 245, 239, 228 CMYK, approx. 0 · 2 · 7 · 4the page. Undyed cotton, honed limestone, where the brands' own logos sit.
Linen#E8DECD · RGB 232, 222, 205 CMYK, approx. 0 · 4 · 12 · 9the second ground, for plates and panels on ivory.
Ink#1B1416 · RGB 27, 20, 22 CMYK, approx. 0 · 26 · 19 · 89text, a warm black with claret in it.
Cypress#1D3A32 · RGB 29, 58, 50 CMYK, approx. 50 · 0 · 14 · 77the deep green of the Levant's trees, for industry, property and people.
Stone#7D7266 · RGB 125, 114, 102 CMYK, approx. 0 · 9 · 18 · 51quiet text and captions on ivory.

Proportions in use: mostly ivory; claret for the house’s own moments; brass only as metal: rules, numerals, the mark. CMYK values are approximate; match on press.

07 · The house canvas

The monogram as a cloth.

Tone on tone, half-drop: for linings, tissue paper, gift boxes, the backs of cards, and quiet bands on screen. Never in brass, never behind text that must be read.

08 · Photography

Material, light and hands.

Warm light, deep shadowOne source of light, falling across the subject; shadows allowed to go dark.
The house’s colours in the frameClaret, ivory, brass, cypress, honey stone. No other brand in the picture.
Hands, not facesCraft shown in the work itself. These are generated stand-ins until the house’s own photographs are taken.

10 · Never

What keeps the mark the mark.

Never stretched or redrawn.

Never in a colour outside the palette.

Never the monogram beside the name in one row.

No shadows, glows, gradients or outlines.
